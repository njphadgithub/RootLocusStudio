Root Locus Theory & Guide

1. Definition & purpose

The root locus shows how closed-loop poles move as gain K changes. Used for stability checks, gain selection, speed/overshoot trade-offs and design.
Characteristic equation: 1 + K·G(s) = 0

2. Basic rules

• Starts at poles (×) when K = 0
• Ends at zeros (○) as K → ∞; the other n−m branches go to ∞ along asymptotes
• Symmetric about the real axis
• Real-axis locus lies left of an odd number of poles+zeros
• Red dots = closed-loop poles at current K
• Yellow square = asymptote centroid; green diamonds = breakaway points; orange rings = jω crossings

3. jω instability

Poles on the jω axis give marginal stability (sustained oscillation). Poles in the RHP give exponentially growing oscillation.

4. Transient performance

ζ sets overshoot, ωₙ sets speed. Poles further left → shorter settling time; poles near the jω axis → larger overshoot.

5. Time-domain metrics

tr 10–90 % rise · tp peak time · Mp overshoot · ts ±2 % settling time.

6. Procedure

1. Pick a preset or type any G(s) (e.g. 10*(s+2)/(s^3+6*s^2+5*s) or [1]/[1 6 8 0]).
2. Press Update Plot.
3. Drag the K slider (or click/drag on the locus) and watch the red poles.
4. Log observations and generate the report.
